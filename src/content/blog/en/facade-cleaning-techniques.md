Not every facade is cleaned the same way. The right technique depends on the material (brick, stone, concrete, glass), the type of soiling (pollution, moss, graffiti) and how sensitive the surface is. Here are the three most commonly used techniques in professional facade cleaning.

## Gentle sandblasting (IBIX sandblaster)

Sandblasting — or dry blasting — projects a fine abrasive at controlled pressure onto the surface. Used with suitable equipment such as an IBIX sandblaster, it allows precise control of pressure and grain size, making it particularly effective for:

- Removing graffiti without attacking the underlying surface;
- Sandblasting soiled stone or brick;
- Targeted, one-off interventions on specific areas.

Its main advantage is precision: unlike standard high-pressure cleaning, it doesn't soak the masonry deeply, which limits the risk of water infiltration.

## Soda blasting

Soda blasting combines water and a fine abrasive projected at low pressure. This technique is gentler than dry blasting and suits fragile or older surfaces (natural stone, listed facades, ornamental details). It also limits dust production — a real advantage in an urban setting or on an occupied site.

## Pure (reverse-osmosis) water

For cleaning windows, conservatories and glazed facades, pure water has become the reference method. Demineralised water leaves no limescale residue as it dries, guaranteeing a streak-free result — even in direct sunlight. Combined with telescopic poles that can reach several dozen metres, it allows glazed facades at height to be cleaned directly from the ground, without a lift or scaffolding, cutting both cost and logistical constraints.

## Which technique for which project?

| Need | Recommended technique |
| --- | --- |
| Graffiti on brick or concrete | Gentle sandblasting |
| Natural stone or an older facade | Soda blasting |
| Windows, conservatories, glazed facades | Pure water + telescopic poles |
| Hard-to-reach facade at height | Lift or boom truck |

## In summary

There is no single "best" facade cleaning method: the right choice depends on the material, the condition of the surface and site accessibility. A prior assessment by a properly equipped professional avoids damaging surfaces while delivering a lasting result.
