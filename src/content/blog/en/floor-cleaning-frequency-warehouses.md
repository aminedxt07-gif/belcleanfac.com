A poorly maintained industrial floor wears out faster, becomes slippery, and can even expose the operator to liability in the event of an accident. Yet there's no single correct frequency — it depends on traffic, the type of activity and the nature of the floor. Here's how to set the right rhythm for your site.

## The factors that determine frequency

- **Traffic**: a hall crossed continuously by forklifts gets dirty far faster than a lightly used storage area.
- **Type of activity**: food, automotive, logistics or events each have different hygiene requirements and different kinds of soiling (oils, dust, food residue).
- **The floor covering**: a raw concrete floor doesn't have the same needs as a resin-coated or treated industrial floor.
- **Regulatory requirements**: some sectors (food, pharmaceutical) impose strict hygiene standards with regular checks.

## Our general recommendations

For a **logistics warehouse with heavy traffic**, running a scrubber several times a week, combined with a thorough monthly deep clean, keeps dust build-up in check and protects the flooring.

For an **industrial or production hall**, frequency depends heavily on the type of soiling generated by the activity: regular degreasing is often needed in addition to routine cleaning.

For an **indoor or outdoor car park**, monthly to bi-monthly maintenance is generally enough, with a reinforced pass after winter to remove salt and grit residue.

For **commercial and office spaces** adjoining an industrial site, a standard weekly clean remains the norm.

## Why regular maintenance costs less than reactive cleaning

A deeply soiled floor requires heavier interventions — intensive degreasing, restoration — which cost more than a well-calibrated regular maintenance plan. A maintenance schedule tailored to your activity also extends the lifespan of the flooring and reduces slip risk, a direct safety issue for your teams and visitors.

## In summary

The right maintenance frequency is defined case by case, based on traffic, activity and your site's flooring. An initial assessment makes it possible to build a tailored maintenance plan, with the right equipment — large-surface scrubbers, high-pressure equipment — and the right intervention rhythm.
